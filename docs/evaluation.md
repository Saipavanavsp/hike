## Summary

- On the holdout set (24 resumes x 34 jobs, every resume read from a PDF or DOCX by the production parser), the Hike hybrid score reaches NDCG@5 0.966, TF-IDF 0.961 and MiniLM alone 0.927.
- Hike vs TF-IDF, NDCG@5: +0.006 (95% CI -0.014 to +0.027): the interval includes zero, so there is no clear difference.
- Hike vs MiniLM, NDCG@5: +0.039 (95% CI +0.015 to +0.065): Hike is better than MiniLM on this data.
- The weakest layout is the two-column PDF: the parser found the candidate's name in 0% of them and missed total experience by 1.1 years on average, because text extraction interleaves the columns.
- The same resume rendered in four layouts moves its match scores by 3.3 points (out of 100) on average, and keeps 92% of its top-5 jobs.
- These numbers come from a small, hand-written dataset with AI-drafted labels. They are evidence that the pipeline works end to end and where it is fragile, not a measurement of real-world hiring quality. See Limitations.

## What was evaluated

Two separate datasets, both fictional:

1. **Synthetic seed set**: 20 resumes x 50 jobs generated by `seed/build_seed_data.py`, with labels from a rule (same role category and experience within range). The system was developed against this data, so it is not a fair test on its own.
2. **Holdout set** (`backend/eval/holdout`): 24 resumes and 34 jobs written by hand, independent of the seed generator, including 6 non-software distractor jobs (sales engineer, business analyst, firmware, Salesforce, technical writer, data entry). Resumes vary in wording ("k8s", "Postgres", "ReactJS"), seniority (intern to staff), and include a career changer and an off-target embedded engineer. Each is rendered in one of four layouts and parsed by the production parser.

Holdout labels are graded: 24 strong fits (2), 45 partial fits (1), and 0 for the other 747 pairs. The rubric:

- **2**: Strong fit: same role family, has the role's primary language/specialisation, covers at least ~60% of the listed skills, and experience is within the range (+/- 1 year).
- **1**: Partial fit: same family sharing at least 1/3 of the listed skills, or an adjacent family sharing at least half; or a strong skills match with experience more than a year outside the range. Judgement is used for career changers.
- **0**: Not a fit (every pair not listed).
- Adjacent role families: fullstack-backend, fullstack-frontend, backend-devops, frontend-mobile.

Label provenance: Drafted with AI assistance (Claude) by applying the rubric to each resume and every job; not yet reviewed by an independent human labeller. Export them for review with: python -m app.ml.eval_holdout --export-labels labels.csv

Metrics, per resume then averaged (95% bootstrap intervals over resumes):

- **P@5 / R@5**: share of the top 5 that is relevant (grade >= 1) / share of relevant jobs found in the top 5.
- **MRR**: 1 / rank of the first relevant job.
- **NDCG@5**: ranking quality with graded gains (2^grade - 1), 1.0 = ideal order.

Each layout holds different resumes with different numbers of acceptable jobs, so P@5 and NDCG@5 differences between rows mostly reflect content, not layout; the format-invariance test below holds content fixed. The parser columns are the layout-specific signal.

Skill recall is measured against each resume's hand-listed skills that exist in the taxonomy (97% of listed skills do). Precision counts any extracted skill not on the hand list as wrong, including more specific ones ("Amazon S3" when the list says "AWS"), so it understates real precision.

### Format invariance

6 resumes were rendered in all four layouts and scored against all jobs. Mean spread of a (resume, job) score across layouts: 3.32 points (worst resume 4.76). Top-5 overlap with the first layout: 92%. Experience-years error by layout: PDF, one column 0.0 yrs, PDF, two columns 1.0 yrs, DOCX, table layout 0.0 yrs, DOCX, all-caps headings 0.0 yrs.

Two-column layouts are the main source of format bias: candidates who use them get a less accurate experience figure, which feeds the experience component of the score. The review screen asks every seeker to confirm or correct the parsed years and skills before matching, which is the mitigation in the product today.

## Limitations

- **No real resumes.** Every resume and job is fictional and written for this project. Real resumes are longer, messier and more varied.
- **Labels are not independent.** They were drafted with AI assistance using the rubric above and have not been checked by a second labeller, so there is no inter-annotator agreement figure.
- **The rubric favours skill overlap.** Grades depend partly on listed-skill overlap, and the Hike score has a skill-overlap component, which flatters Hike relative to the baselines.
- **Small sample.** 24 resumes give wide confidence intervals; category rows have 1-4 resumes each.
- **Ceiling effects.** MRR is 1.0 for every method, and NDCG@5 is near its ceiling.
- **No demographic fairness test.** The data has no consented demographic attributes, so this report covers format robustness only, not bias across candidate groups.

## What a real-world evaluation would need

1. Consented, anonymised resumes and job posts (names, contacts and photos removed before labelling).
2. At least two independent labellers per pair, the rubric above, and Cohen's kappa reported.
3. A few hundred resumes, stratified by role, seniority and layout.
4. Outcome labels where available (shortlisted / interviewed) alongside judged relevance.

The harness already supports this: put the files in `backend/eval/holdout`, export pairs with `--export-labels`, collect grades, import them with `--import-labels`, and rerun with `--report`.
